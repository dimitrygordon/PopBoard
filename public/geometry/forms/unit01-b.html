Show every reason. A drawing is not evidence unless the problem gives it.

Original aligned items. Related scope: Jacobs Introduction and Chapters 1 and 2.

1.

A circle has two chords that look equal, with no marks. Eli writes 'the chords are congruent because the picture is symmetric.' What information, if it were given, would make that a fair given? What is still unproved in the drawing as it is?

2.

A sequence starts at u0 = 2, and each term is the one before plus 3: u(n + 1) = u(n) + 3 for every whole number n. Find u3. Why is this deduction and not a guess from a pattern?

3.

Four survey markers are all different, and no three of them are on one line. A report says only five lines are needed to connect every pair of markers. Show the report is wrong without measuring any angles.

4.

On one round-planet model, a 9 degree north-south arc is 1,000 km long. On another it is 1,500 km. Using the same 'equal arc for each degree' rule, which planet is bigger around, and what is the ratio?

5.

A simple five-sided region has B and D on its boundary. Point W lies between B and D but outside the region; these facts are given. Is the region convex? Which part of the definition fails?

6.

Circle c has center P and passes through Q, where P and Q are different. Circle d has center Q and passes through P. The two radii are equal. Are c and d the same circle? Decide by checking whether P is on each one.

7.

A system's only assumptions are 'A implies B' and 'B implies C.' Show that A implies C. Must 'C implies A' also follow? Give true or false values for A, B and C to back up your answer.

8.

A device rule says 'if the switch is on, then the lamp is on.' Four observations of (switch, lamp) are (off, off), (off, on), (on, on) and (on, off). Which one breaks the rule? Does seeing the lamp on prove the switch is on?

9.

A database calls a pair of real numbers (x, y) 'reciprocal.' Its test is x + y = 1. Find a pair that passes the test but shouldn't, a pair that fails but should pass, and the correct exact test.

10.

Solve x² = x for real numbers. A classmate divides both sides by x right away and gets only x = 1. Find every solution and the case that was lost.

11.

You are given two rules: 'H implies E' and 'H implies not E.' Prove 'not H' by contradiction. Don't assume E or not E before you start.

12.

System U assumes exactly one line through every two distinct points. System E assumes at least one line through every two distinct points and does not assume uniqueness. (In plain words: For every pair of different points, U requires exactly one line through both points. E requires at least one line through both points, but allows either one or more than one.) Does 'two different lines can't share two different points' hold in both systems? If not, build a small example that breaks it.
