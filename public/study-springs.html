Hooke's law is a model with a range: inside the proportional limit, F ∝ x and unloading retraces the line to zero. Past the elastic limit the specimen keeps a permanent deformation when the force is removed. Never quote these illustrative limits as properties of a real spring — measure the real one.

The Big Idea

One law, four faces. F = −kx says a spring's restoring force is proportional to displacement and always aimed at equilibrium. Graph it and you get a straight line whose slope is −k. Hang a mass and equilibrium is where that force grows to match weight, kx = mg. Release a displaced mass and the same proportionality makes acceleration proportional to −x — the defining condition for simple harmonic motion, with T = 2π√(m/k) set by inertia and stiffness alone, never by amplitude. Push too far and the model itself expires: past the proportional and elastic limits the line bends and the spring may not come back. Springs combine like the extensions and forces they share — series adds extensions (softer), parallel adds forces (stiffer) — and damping and driving decide whether the oscillation rings, creeps, or resonates.
