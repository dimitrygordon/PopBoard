The Big Idea

A vector is fully described by its components. Tip-to-tail addition and component addition are the same addition in two outfits: draw it and you see the resultant; split into Fx = F·cosθ and Fy = F·sinθ, add the columns, rebuild with R = √(Rx²+Ry²) and θ = atan2(Ry, Rx), and you get a number you can trust to a decimal place. The equilibrant is just the resultant with the opposite sign — add it and the forces balance, which by Newton's 1st law means no change in motion. On a ramp, gravity itself gets the component treatment: mg·sinθ pulls down the plane, mg·cosθ presses into it (and is what the normal force answers), and slip begins exactly when the down-plane part outgrows μs·FN.
