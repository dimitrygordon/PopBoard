RBMK Reactor Simulator ЯБМК

A two-zone classroom model of an RBMK-1000, the Chernobyl Unit 4 type: 3200 MWt, graphite moderator fixed in the stack, water as coolant and neutron absorber. The same physics runs the sandbox and the night of 25–26 April 1986. A pressure failure here is a steam explosion, not a nuclear detonation. Peak power in that failure reads OFF SCALE. The particle view is an illustration of that model. It does not feed it.

Inventory

Uranium, inert products, and the graphite stack stay fixed on this night. Iodine and xenon are multiples of their full-power equilibrium. Water is the liquid fraction of the coolant (1 − void).

Controls

Digits on the tiles carry a little sensor noise. Charts and the reactivity table use the model. Noise never goes back into the physics.

Control-rod bank insertion

Those three groups existed on a real RBMK. In this model they move as one bank. Insertion percent is the mechanical stroke. ORM is absorber-equivalent and is not the same number: on the night line the absorbers are still above the fuel.

Sandbox only. The loop reads smoothed power, waits one second, and moves at most 5% of the stroke per second. It ignores a band of ±3%. The 20 and 40 marks on the particle view are a display key, not this setpoint. Off during the Chernobyl night.

About one spent bundle a few seconds, fission products only. The tally is on the particle view. Off during the Chernobyl night, so burnup stays where the story left it.

AZ-5 is the scram. It was the correct button. Rods take about 20 seconds, and they are not magic: from a fully withdrawn line the graphite followers enter the core before the absorbers. That positive bump is a design flaw, not sabotage.

Main circulation pumps

Eight pumps. Six running is the 100% flow used at full power. Switching a pump off coasts it down over about 30–60 seconds. Flow is one number for the whole core.

Time scale

1× is real time. 60× and 600× are for the hours xenon actually takes. The rundown stays at 1× so the coast-down can be watched.

What is fact, reconstruction, and unknown

Fact

Chernobyl Unit 4 was an RBMK-1000. A steam explosion destroyed the reactor and lifted the upper shield. Graphite is the moderator and stays in the stack, except the graphite followers on the control rods. Water is the coolant and a neutron absorber. The void coefficient was positive. The operators pressed AZ-5.

Reconstruction

Power was cut from 3200 MWt toward the test range, then held for hours near 1600 MWt. After midnight it fell to tens of megawatts and was recovered only to about 200 MWt, with the operating reactivity margin near 8. Extra pumps were on for the test. Four coasted down. AZ-5 was pressed as power rose.

Unknown

The precise peak power. This module will not invent one. A second explosion followed seconds later and exposed the core. Its mechanism is disputed: more steam, hydrogen, or another power pulse. The module shows the steam explosion and an exposed core, and it labels the second blast as disputed.

Accuracy traps

  • There are no moderator rods. Graphite does not move, except the control-rod followers.
  • Water is not the main moderator. Losing water adds reactivity in this design.
  • Scram is not instant. AZ-5 was not sabotage.
  • Xenon-135 builds and decays over hours, not minutes. Iodine-135 is 6.6 h. Xenon-135 is 9.2 h.
  • Burnup and inert products stay frozen. The fuel does not melt first.
  • Nothing here is a nuclear weapon. There is no mushroom cloud and no green “radiation” glow. Phosphor green is only for instrument digits.
