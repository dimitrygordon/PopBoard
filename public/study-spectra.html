The Big Idea

Continuous, emission, and absorption spectra are three views of the same physics. A dense hot object emits a continuous blackbody-like spectrum whose peak reveals its temperature (Wien) and whose total power follows σT4. A thin excited gas emits only at discrete wavelengths; a thin cooler gas in front of a continuum absorbs at exactly those same wavelengths. Matching those fingerprints — shifted by redshift if the source moves — lets astronomers measure the temperatures, compositions, and motions of objects they can never touch.
