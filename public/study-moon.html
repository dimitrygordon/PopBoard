The Big Idea — and the Big Misconception

Phases are NOT Earth’s shadow. The Sun always illuminates about half of the Moon. As the Moon orbits Earth (about 29.5 days from new moon to new moon), we see more or less of that sunlit half: at new moon the lit half faces away from us, at full moon it faces straight at us, and at the quarter moons we see exactly half of it lit. Earth’s shadow only touches the Moon during a lunar eclipse — and that does not happen most full moons, because the Moon’s orbit is tilted about 5°, so it usually passes a little above or below the shadow (see the side view above).

Waxing means the lit part is growing (lit on the right for Northern Hemisphere observers); waning means it is shrinking (lit on the left). A handy check: the lit edge always points toward the Sun.
