A small %-change activity: Δm = −2.5 log₁₀(1 + x), where x is the fractional change. A +10% brightness bump is only about −0.10 mag — magnitudes compress changes.

Magnitude Scale Explorer

Smaller (even negative) = brighter · 5 mag = exactly 100×

Hipparchus (about 150 BCE) sorted visible stars into six classes: 1st magnitude brightest, 6th faintest. In 1856 Norman Pogson made it quantitative: a difference of 5 magnitudes = a factor of exactly 100 in brightness, so 1 mag = 1001/5 ≈ 2.512×. The scale runs backwards and past zero — Sirius is m ≈ −1.46 and the Sun is m ≈ −26.7.

Extension — interstellar dust
Extinction A: 0.0 mag

—

Dust dims a star by A magnitudes: mobserved = m + A. If you ignore dust, you overestimate the distance by a factor 10A/5 — the star only looks farther away. Errors work the same way: a 0.5 mag error in m or M shifts the inferred distance by about 26%.

Blended Starlight Challenge

Unresolved stars add in brightness — never in magnitude
—

Recipe: convert each magnitude to a linear brightness, b = 10−0.4m; add the b values; convert back, mcombined = −2.5 log₁₀(btotal). Two stars at m = 0 blend to m ≈ −0.75, not 0 and not −0 or 0+0.

Brightest-looking vs. most luminous

Sirius looks brightest in our night sky (m ≈ −1.46), but it is only about 25 L☉ and very nearby. Betelgeuse looks fainter (m ≈ +0.4) yet radiates tens of thousands of times more light — it is simply far away. Which is which?

The Big Idea

Luminosity is what a star emits; brightness is what we receive. The same luminosity L spreads over a sphere of area 4πd², so the received brightness is F = L/(4πd²) — the inverse-square law. Double the distance and F drops to 1/4; triple it and F drops to 1/9. Magnitudes are a logarithmic shorthand for F: smaller is brighter, 5 steps are 100×, and absolute magnitude M simply asks, “what m would this star have at the standard distance of 10 pc?” That one question separates how a star looks from how powerful it is — and, run backwards through the distance modulus, it turns a brightness measurement into a distance.
