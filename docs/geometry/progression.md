# Geometry: Full-Year Course Progression (PopBoard)

Prepared for: Dimitry Gordon, student teacher, Jasper High School (Indiana), shadowing Mrs. Painter
Format: 180 school days, 50-minute classes, 12 units, 36 weeks
Standards: 2023 Indiana Academic Standards for Geometry (IDOE PDF: https://media.doe.in.gov/standards/indiana-academic-standards-geometry.pdf). Codes marked (E) are essential standards.
Companion text: Harold R. Jacobs, *Geometry: Seeing, Doing, Understanding*, 3rd ed. (2003). Chapter numbers below refer to that book.
Visual style: black-and-white line art, compass-and-straightedge constructions, fractals and patterns.

## How to read this
- Days are lesson days. One lesson = one 50-minute class.
- "Interactive: construction" = a web interactive where students build a figure step by step with compass and straightedge tools.
- "Interactive: sandbox" = a free-play dynamic geometry space where students drag, measure, and test conjectures.
- Lessons without a flag work best on paper, patty paper, or in discussion.

## A note on the standard codes
The 2023 Indiana Geometry standards use five domains: G.GF (Geometry Foundations, 1 to 7), G.T (Triangles, 1 to 7), G.QP (Quadrilaterals and Other Polygons, 1 to 4), G.CI (Circles, 1 to 3), G.TS (Transformations and Three-Dimensional Solids, 1 to 5). G.T.1 has lettered parts a to i. The codes G.LP, G.PL, G.TR, and G.RT are from the 2020 standards and do not appear in the 2023 document, so they are not used here. The 2026 standards (to be implemented in 2027-2028) were not used.

## Design rules for the sequence
1. Congruence is transformation-based throughout. Congruent means "related by a sequence of rigid motions" (G.TS.1, G.T.2). SAS is established from rigid motions, then ASA, then SSS is proved from SAS. Similar means "related by a dilation plus rigid motions" (G.TS.2, G.T.3).
2. Nothing is used before it is proved or postulated. Exterior angle inequality, triangle inequalities, and the tests for parallel lines (via the exterior angle inequality) come before the parallel postulate. The 180 degree angle sum, AAS, and HL come after it.
3. SSA is never a congruence test (a counterexample is shown on purpose). HL is valid for right triangles only. AAA gives similarity only.
4. Area comes before similarity. The Pythagorean Theorem is proved first by area, then again by similarity. The distance formula comes only after the Pythagorean Theorem.
5. Trig ratios are introduced as consequences of similar right triangles.
6. Circles come after similarity. Circumference and circle area are built from regular polygons after trig. Pi is irrational; 3.14 and 22/7 are approximations only.
7. Volume: Cavalieri's principle first, then the prism formula, then everything else.
8. Non-Euclidean content is enrichment only.

## Unit 1: Seeing and Reasoning (3 weeks, 14 days)
- Big idea: Pictures and measurements suggest; only proof convinces. Essential question: How do we know something in geometry is true?
- Standards: G.GF.1 (E), G.GF.2, G.GF.3
- Jacobs: Introduction (Euclid, the Surfer, and the Spotter), Inductive Reasoning, Ch 1 (An Introduction to Geometry), Ch 2 (The Nature of Deductive Reasoning)
- Prior knowledge: angle and length measurement, basic shape vocabulary, solving one-step equations
- Misconceptions: a few examples prove a rule; a drawing that looks right is right; converse of a true statement is automatically true; definitions are "just vocabulary" rather than reversible statements

## Unit 2: Lines, Angles, and Constructions (3 weeks, 15 days)
- Big idea: A few postulates about rulers and protractors produce reliable theorems about angles. Essential question: What can we prove from measuring?
- Standards: G.GF.1 (E), G.GF.3, G.GF.4 (E), G.GF.6
- Jacobs: Ch 3 (Lines and Angles), 1.4, 4.7 (Constructions)
- Working definition: for now, congruent segments are segments of equal length and congruent angles are angles of equal measure (refined by rigid motions in Unit 3).
- Prior knowledge: properties of equality, solving linear equations, using a protractor
- Misconceptions: congruent and equal are interchangeable in every context; vertical angles are any angles "across"; a perpendicular bisector is any perpendicular line; a construction is the same as a drawing measured with a ruler

## Unit 3: Rigid Motions and Symmetry (3 weeks, 14 days)
- Big idea: Sliding, flipping, and turning keep shape and size. Essential question: How can we tell, without measuring everything, that two figures are the same?
- Standards: G.TS.1 (E), G.GF.6
- Jacobs: Ch 8 (Transformations), 6.1 (Line Symmetry), 7.2 (Point Symmetry). Taught earlier than in Jacobs so congruence can be defined by rigid motions.
- Prior knowledge: coordinate plane, grade 8 transformations, perpendicular bisector construction
- Misconceptions: a rotation is always about the origin; a reflection line must be vertical or horizontal; order of transformations never matters; a translation "moves the shape" rather than every point

## Unit 4: Triangle Congruence (3 weeks, 16 days)
- Big idea: Three well-chosen parts are enough to pin down a triangle. Essential question: What is the least information that guarantees two triangles are congruent?
- Standards: G.T.2 (E), G.T.3, G.T.1b, G.GF.3, G.GF.6
- Jacobs: Ch 4 (Congruence: 4.2 to 4.7). Jacobs postulates ASA and SAS; here SAS and ASA are justified from rigid motions (Indiana approach), and SSS is proved from SAS following Jacobs 4.6.
- Prior knowledge: rigid motions preserve length and angle (Unit 3); reflections across perpendicular bisectors; angle bisectors
- Misconceptions: SSA (and AAA) prove congruence; corresponding parts can be matched in any order; CPCTC can be used before triangles are proved congruent

## Unit 5: Inequalities and Parallel Lines (3.5 weeks, 17 days)
- Big idea: Some facts need no parallel postulate; the 180 degree angle sum does. Essential question: What changes once we assume the parallel postulate?
- Standards: G.T.1g, G.T.1h, G.T.1i, G.GF.4 (E), G.GF.5 (E), G.T.1a, G.T.2 (AAS, HL)
- Jacobs: Ch 5 (Inequalities), Ch 6 (Parallel Lines). Order follows Jacobs: inequalities and parallel tests first, then the Parallel Postulate, then angle sum, AAS, HL.
- Prior knowledge: SAS, isosceles theorems, vertical angles, slope from Algebra I
- Misconceptions: the longest side can be anywhere; any three lengths make a triangle; the converse of a parallel-line theorem is the same theorem; HL works for any triangle; perpendicular slopes are just "negatives"

- Big idea: Dilations change size but keep shape, so ratios are preserved. Essential question: What stays the same when a figure is scaled?
- Standards: G.TS.2, G.T.3, G.T.4 (E), G.T.1e, G.T.1f
