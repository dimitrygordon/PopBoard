## 4.9

### Items

4.9-1: L↔W, so angle W; K↔U and J↔V, so side UV.
Second check: Reverse the lists VUW and JKL: W returns to L and UV returns to KJ.

4.9-2: N↔Z and P↔X, so NP=ZX. Solve 4t+5=37, 4t=32, t=8.
Second check: Substitute: 4(8)+5=37. Both first-to-third sides have length 37.

4.9-3: RS=XY and RT=XZ are given. The equal angles are included, so RST≅XYZ by SAS. ST=YZ by CPCTC.
Second check: R↔X, S↔Y, T↔Z; the paired sides meet at R and X, exactly the marked angle vertices.

4.9-4: ASA proves EFG≅LMN. FG and MN join the two given angle vertices, so they are included sides.
Second check: Map F to M and G to N; the endpoint rays then determine E and L uniquely on the chosen side.

4.9-5: RH=PH=6 and SH=QH=10 by midpoint. Angle PHQ=RHS by vertical angles. Hence PHQ≅RHS by SAS.
Second check: The half-turn about H sends P to R and Q to S, giving an independent motion check of the same correspondence.

4.9-6: U↔K and W↔L, so UW=KL. Thus 2r+9=35, 2r=26, r=13.
Second check: 2(13)+9=35; read first-to-third positions in both ordered triples.

4.9-7: Equal legs give angle E=angle F, so 5z+1=3z+19, 2z=18, z=9. Each base angle is 46 degrees.
Second check: 5(9)+1=46 and 3(9)+19=46. Both are positive and less than 90; no angle sum is used in the student solution.

4.9-8: The converse isosceles theorem gives JL=JK. Solve 6s-7=4s+9, 2s=16, s=8; both sides are 41.
Second check: 6(8)-7=41=4(8)+9. Independently compare JLK and JKL: equal base angles and LK=KL give ASA, then CPCTC.

4.9-9: The claimed correspondence would require CD=ST, but 4≠10. Equal angles do not force congruence.
Second check: All three angle sizes are distinct, so any congruence must match equal-sized angles in exactly this order. No alternative vertex matching rescues the claim.

4.9-10: First (iii), by reflexivity. Combine it with the two givens to obtain (ii), by SAS. Only then conclude (i), by CPCTC.
Second check: C is the included-angle vertex between AC and CD, and between BC and CD. The CPCTC line now has its congruence hypothesis established.

## 4.16

### A

A-1: Side JK and angle H, by the ordered correspondence.
Second check: Read HJK back against TUV: both requested matches reverse to the original parts.

A-2: LM=CB by CPCTC, so 7a-6=57, 7a=63, a=9 and LM=57.
Second check: 7(9)-6=57. L↔C and M↔B check the matched endpoints.

A-3: The two side pairs and their included angles match, so HIJ≅RST by SAS. Then IJ=ST by CPCTC.
Second check: The side endpoints share H and R, the marked angle vertices. A ray-alignment rigid motion fixes I to S and J to T.

A-4: VW and FE are included sides between the marked angles, so UVW≅DFE by ASA. UW matches DE.
Second check: V↔F and W↔E fix the base endpoints; the two endpoint rays intersect uniquely, forcing U↔D.

A-5: JO=KO=4 and LO=NO=9 by midpoint. Angle JOL=KON by vertical angles. JOL≅KON by SAS.
Second check: A half-turn about O exchanges J,K and L,N; it preserves all distances and gives the same congruence.

A-6: G↔T, so 3c+8=50 by CPCTC. Hence 3c=42 and c=14.
Second check: 3(14)+8=50; the second letters in FGH and UTS match.

A-7: The isosceles theorem gives 2e+14=5e-16. Thus 3e=30, e=10 and both base angles are 34 degrees.
Second check: 2(10)+14=34 and 5(10)-16=34, with positive equal legs possible. Do not compute the apex angle.

A-8: Compare VXW and VWX. The base-angle pairs match and XW=WX; ASA gives congruence and CPCTC gives VX=VW. Solve 3g+4=g+32, so g=14 and each leg is 46.
Second check: 3(14)+4=46=14+32. Also the established converse isosceles theorem directly gives the same leg equality.

A-9: The unique equal-angle matching is A↔U, B↔V, C↔W. It would require AB=UV, but 6≠15, so the triangles are not congruent.
Second check: Rigid motions preserve lengths, and the distinct angle sizes forbid any different vertex matching. The angle equalities are givens, not an inference from equilateral triangles.

A-10: First KM=KM by reflexivity. With KL=KN and LM=NM, KLM≅KNM by SSS. Now angle LKM=NKM by CPCTC. In a convex quadrilateral the diagonal KM lies inside angle LKN, so it bisects that angle.
Second check: K↔K, L↔N, M↔M matches each of the three side facts. Both equal angles are the two parts of the original angle, not exterior angles.

A-11: EG=EH=10 and FG=FH=10 by equal radii; EF=EF, so EFG≅EFH by SSS. Radius 7 gives tangency at the midpoint, not two points, because 7+7=14. The perpendicular-bisector construction needs radius greater than 7.
Second check: Reflection across EF fixes the centers and swaps G,H.

A-12: OP is common, PQ1=PQ2 as radii of the same circle, and angle POQ1=POQ2 because OQ1,OQ2 are the same ray. This angle is not included between OP and PQ. The third sides are 2 and 10, so the named correspondence fails congruence.
Second check: Writing the unknown radius as r, the perimeters are 12+r and 20+r, unequal. Thus no other correspondence can make the triangles congruent.

### B

B-1: Side QR and angle P, by the ordered correspondence.
Second check: Read QPR back against WXY: both requested matches reverse to the original parts.

B-2: EG=RP by CPCTC, so 6b+5=71, 6b=66, b=11 and EG=71.
Second check: 6(11)+5=71. E↔R and G↔P check the first-to-third endpoints.

B-3: KL=VW, KM=VZ and their included angles are equal, so KLM≅VWZ by SAS. LM=WZ by CPCTC.
Second check: K↔V, L↔W, M↔Z gives both side pairs and the angle at their common endpoint. Aligning those rays fixes all vertices.

B-4: OP and SR join the marked-angle vertices. Thus NOP≅LSR by ASA, and NP matches LR.
Second check: O↔S, P↔R force N↔L by the unique intersection of the endpoint rays, independently confirming correspondence.

B-5: AZ=CZ=7 and BZ=DZ=12 by midpoint. Angle AZB=CZD by vertical angles. AZB≅CZD by SAS.
Second check: A half-turn about Z sends A to C and B to D, confirming the claimed matching without a triangle inequality or parallel-line theorem.

B-6: N↔W, so 5d-9=76 by CPCTC. Thus 5d=85 and d=17.
Second check: 5(17)-9=76; N and W are both third letters.

B-7: The isosceles theorem gives 4f-11=2f+15. Hence 2f=26, f=13, and both base angles are 41 degrees.
Second check: 4(13)-11=41 and 2(13)+15=41. The base-angle theorem, not an angle-sum calculation, supplies the equation.

B-8: Compare HIK and HKI. Equal base-angle pairs and IK=KI give ASA, then HI=HK by CPCTC. Solve 5h-8=2h+43, so h=17 and each leg is 77.
Second check: 5(17)-8=77=2(17)+43. The proved converse isosceles theorem is an independent direct check of the equality.

B-9: The unique equal-angle matching is D↔L, E↔M, F↔N. Congruence would require DE=LM; 8≠20 rules it out.
Second check: Any isometry preserves the given side length. Because the angle sizes are distinct, permuting vertices cannot bypass that mismatch.

B-10: First RT=RT by reflexivity. Then RST≅RUT by SSS using the two given pairs. CPCTC gives angle SRT=URT. Convexity puts RT inside angle SRU, hence RT is its bisector.
Second check: The ordered match R↔R, S↔U, T↔T makes all three SSS equalities corresponding. The conclusion occurs only after congruence.

B-11: GJ=GK=12 and HJ=HK=12 by equal radii; GH=GH, so GHJ≅GHK by SSS. Radius 9 gives tangency, since 9+9=18. Two intersections require radius greater than 9.
Second check: Reflection across GH exchanges J,K.

B-12: LM is shared; MN1=MN2 as radii of one circle; angle MLN1=MLN2 because both N points are on the same ray from L. That angle is nonincluded for LM and MN. LN1=5 differs from LN2=11, ruling out the named congruence.
Second check: With unknown common radius r, perimeters 22+r and 28+r differ, so no alternative correspondence works either.
